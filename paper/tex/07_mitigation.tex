\section{Mitigation: Change the Contract, Not the Players}
\label{sec:mitigation}

If asymmetric exposure is equilibrium behaviour, mitigation cannot
consist of detecting deviants---at equilibrium there are none. Two
repair strategies remain: discipline the \emph{reports} (mechanism
design on the budget channel) or discipline the \emph{contract} (bound
what any profile can do to a player). The first is unavailable; the
second provably works.

\paragraph{No report-based fix.} Viewed as a direct-revelation
mechanism on the budget channel, sampling-based \ac{iDP} without transfers
is not dominant-strategy incentive-compatible: a player's
utility-maximising budget report necessarily depends on the others'
declarations.

\begin{proposition}[Non-strategyproofness of budget declaration]
\label[proposition]{prop:non-strategyproof}
For any player $i$ with $\lambda_i > 0$ there exist opponent declaration
profiles $\hat{\boldsymbol{\eps}}_{-i}^{\mathrm{L}}$,
$\hat{\boldsymbol{\eps}}_{-i}^{\mathrm{T}}$ such that player $i$'s best-response
reports against them differ. In particular, no report is a dominant
strategy, and the identity mechanism on the budget channel is not
DSIC.
\end{proposition}

The proof (\cref{app:mechanism-design}) chains
\cref{lem:externality} through the utility gradient and exhibits a
numerical witness: against a loose opponent the best report is
$\hat\eps_1 \approx 2.9$, against a tight one $\approx 1.2$. The
classical rescues do not apply---the large-market route
of~\cite{kearns2014mechanism} needs each agent's influence on the
mechanism to vanish, whereas each silo moves (Batch) at constant
magnitude, and the truthful-elicitation results
of~\cite{ghosh2011selling, chen2013truthful, nissim2014redrawing}
model privacy costs that vanish without influence, whereas here every
report moves $\sigma$. \Cref{app:mechanism-design} sets up the
type-channel (Myerson-style) design problem this opens and why it is
harder than an auction: a shared $\sigma$, interim advantages coupled
through all types, and no monetary transfer.

\paragraph{The contract-based fix.} The
$(\eps_i,\delta,\Delta)$-iDP contract of \citet{kaiser2026your,
kaissis2024beyond} adds one clause to the individual contract: the
realised mechanism must stay within $\Delta$-divergence of a
reference, equivalently the slack of \eqref{eq:decomp} is capped.
In the vocabulary of this paper, the clause is exactly an externality
bound:

\begin{proposition}[$\Delta$-neutralisation of the externality]
\label[proposition]{prop:delta-neutralisation}
Restrict the action space to profiles with
$\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps})\leq\Delta$ for every player.
Then at every feasible profile
\[
A_{\eps_i,\delta}-\Delta \;\leq\; \Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \;\leq\;
A_{\eps_i,\delta},
\]
and for any two feasible profiles agreeing in player $i$'s own budget,
$\bigl|\Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps})-\Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps}')\bigr|\leq\Delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both claims are immediate from the identity \eqref{eq:decomp} and
$\mathrm{slack}_i \in [0, \Delta]$: the realised advantage is the
ceiling minus the slack, and two profiles sharing $\eps_i$ share the
ceiling, so their advantage difference is a difference of slacks.
\end{proof}

The proposition is deliberately elementary---that is its virtue. In
the terms of the hierarchy \eqref{eq:hierarchy}, the $\Delta$-clause
pins the distance between levels 1 and 2: the level-2 advantage may
sit at most $\Delta$ below the level-1 ceiling, so no unilateral or
joint deviation by the other players, coordinated or not, can move
player $i$'s realised advantage by more than $\Delta$ at fixed
$\eps_i$. The coalition curve of \cref{fig:C4} is capped at height
$\Delta$ above its floor, and the strategic redistribution of
\cref{sec:C1} is bounded to a $\Delta$-band around each contract.
What $\Delta$-iDP does \emph{not} automatically deliver is a
well-behaved residual game---whether an equilibrium of the restricted
game exists, is unique, or is symmetric, and what utility cost the
restriction imposes through forced noise, are open (we return to
this in \cref{sec:discussion}). The equilibrium analysis of this
paper is the reason to pay that cost: without the clause, the
contract's realised meaning is set by everyone else's play.
