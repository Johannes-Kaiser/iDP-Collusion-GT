\section{Equilibrium Analysis: Someone Must Carry the Risk}
\label{sec:analysis}

As orientation before the details, the section's argument in brief:
the externality is monotone; monotonicity rules out symmetric pure
equilibria; preference heterogeneity then decides who carries the
exposed role; equilibria exist in the practically relevant regime,
though the game admits no potential function; and coalition play
recovers the known attack. The subsections develop these claims in
exactly this order; full proofs are deferred to \cref{app:proofs},
and the short ones stay inline.

\subsection{The externality is monotone}

Player $i$'s risk depends on exactly two quantities, $(p_i, \sigma)$,
and the mechanism sets both from the whole budget vector. The first
lemma fixes the \emph{direction} in which the other players' choices
move them.

\begin{lemma}[Privacy externality on the feasible region]
\label[lemma]{lem:externality}
Consider the sampling-based \ac{iDP} mechanism of \cref{def:sampling-idp}
on a feasible profile $\boldsymbol{\eps}$ at which constraints (Budget)
bind for every group and (Batch) binds at $B$. Then for every pair $i
\neq j$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial \eps_j} \;<\; 0
\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial \eps_j} \;<\; 0.
\label{eq:externality-lemma}
\end{equation}
When player $j$ loosens their budget, the mechanism uses less noise and
samples everyone else less.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
The externality travels through the only two quantities the players
share: the batch and the noise. Amplification makes each group's
binding rate $p_g(\sigma, \eps_g)$ increase in its own budget and in
the shared $\sigma$~\cite{balle2020privacy}, so the expected batch
is strictly increasing in $\sigma$ and (Batch) pins a unique
$\sigma$. When player $j$ loosens their budget, they claim a larger
share of the fixed batch; the solver can restore $B$ only by
lowering $\sigma$, and the lower noise shrinks every other player's
binding rate. Player $i$'s mechanism has moved---in both
dials---though $\eps_i$ never did. Full proof in
\cref{app:proof-externality}.
\end{proof}

The lemma leaves the sign of the externality genuinely undecided:
when player $j$ loosens their budget, player $i$ is sampled less
(safer) under less noise (more exposed), and the two channels race.
The next result settles the race where \ac{DPSGD} actually
operates---small sampling rates, where player $i$'s advantage is
essentially linear in their rate. There, the protection from being
sampled less strictly outweighs the exposure from the thinner noise,
at every $\sigma$: tightening a budget always exports risk onto the
others.

\begin{corollary}[Externality on $\Apld{i}$, small-$p$ regime]
\label[corollary]{cor:externality-small-p}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:externality}, assume additionally
$p_g \leq p_\star$ for every group, with $p_\star$ small enough that
the $O(p^2)$ remainder of the subsampling
expansion~\cite{balle2020privacy} is dominated by the leading term.
Then $\Apld{i}(p_i,\sigma) = I\, p_i\, \Adv_G(\sigma) + O\bigl((I
p_i)^2\bigr)$ with $\Adv_G(\sigma) = 2\Phi(1/(2\sigma))-1$, and
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \Apld{i}}{\partial \eps_j}
   \;=\; \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial \eps_j}
        \cdot I\, p_i \cdot
        \Bigl[\, \tfrac{\Adv_G(\sigma)}{\sigma}
              + \Adv_G'(\sigma)\, \Bigr] \;+\; O\bigl((I p_i)^2\bigr)
\;<\; 0
\label{eq:proof-bracket}
\end{equation}
for every $i \neq j$ on the small-$p$ face of the feasible region.
The regime is $I p_i \ll 1$: the expansion is exact per composition
step and asymptotic for the composed mechanism, whose operating
points ($I p_i$ of order one to one hundred) are covered by the
certificate of \cref{prop:externality-full} instead.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
The Poisson-subsampled trade-off function is the mixture
$T_{p,\sigma} = (1-p)(1-\alpha) + p\,T_\sigma(\alpha) + O(p^2)$, so
its sup-gap is $p\,\Adv_G(\sigma) + O(p^2)$. The bracket in
\eqref{eq:proof-bracket} is $\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{d}{d\sigma}[\sigma
\Adv_G(\sigma)]$, strictly positive (\cref{app:proofs}); multiplying
by $\partial\sigma/\partial\eps_j < 0$ closes the sign.
\end{proof}

For what follows we need the mechanism solution in closed form. In
\ac{RDP} form~\cite{mironov2017renyi}, (Budget) reads $p_g(\sigma,
\eps_g) = \sigma\sqrt{\rho_g(\eps_g,\delta)}/\sqrt{2I\alpha}$ up to
higher order in $p_g$, so (Batch) becomes the aggregate identity
\begin{equation}
\sigma \cdot \sum_{g}\, |G_g|\, \sqrt{\rho_g(\eps_g,\delta)} \;=\; B
\sqrt{2I\alpha}.
\label{eq:sigma-implicit}
\end{equation}
Beyond the small-$p$ face---at the composed operating points $I p_i$
of order one to one hundred---the expansion no longer decides the
sign, but a structural reduction explains what an $\eps_j$-move can
and cannot do to player $i$. Because $i$'s (Budget) constraint stays
binding, the move slides $i$'s mechanism along the ray $p_i \propto
\sigma$, on which its leading-order (R\'enyi) privacy content is
constant: the mechanism is built to hold every nominal guarantee
fixed, so at the resolution of the contract the externality is
exactly invisible. Everything happens in the higher-order shape of
the profile---which is why the effect accumulates \emph{below} the
contract, as slack, rather than ever violating it, and why a
residual this fine resists a full analytic closure. We certify its
sign exhaustively instead.

\begin{proposition}[Full-region sign: reduction and certificate]
\label[proposition]{prop:externality-full}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:externality}, with strict interior
$p_g(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \in (0,1)$ for every group: (i) at fixed
$\eps_i$, the mechanism holds player $i$'s leading-order (R\'enyi)
privacy content invariant in $\eps_j$, so the cross-effect on $\Apld{i}$
is carried entirely by higher-order terms of the privacy profile;
(ii) the sign of that residual is $\partial \Apld{i} / \partial \eps_j <
0$---analytically in the small-$p$ regime
(\cref{cor:externality-small-p}), and by numerical certificate on
$99.1\%$ of the interior cells of the $K{=}200$ mechanism grid
(\cref{app:certificate}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Part (i) is the ray invariance just described---by
\eqref{eq:sigma-implicit}, (Budget) gives $p_i \propto \sigma$ at
fixed $\eps_i$---made exact through a sandwich of Gaussian trade-off
envelopes in \cref{app:proof-full-region}. Part (ii) combines the
bracket of \eqref{eq:proof-bracket} on the small-$p$ face with the
finite-difference certificate on the rest (\cref{app:certificate});
the failing $0.9\%$ of cells sit exactly where the ray argument
itself breaks, at the saturation corner and the feasibility edge.
\end{proof}

In the decomposition \eqref{eq:decomp}, the same fact becomes a
statement about the hierarchy \eqref{eq:hierarchy}: the ceiling
$A_{\eps_i,\delta}$ never moves---only the contract sets it---while
the advantage beneath it does, and only the \emph{other} players move
it.

\begin{proposition}[Slack monotonicity]
\label[proposition]{prop:slack-monotonicity}
On the strict interior of the feasible region, for every $i \neq j$,
\[
\frac{\partial \mathrm{slack}_i}{\partial \eps_j}(\boldsymbol{\eps})
\;=\; -\,\frac{\partial \Apld{i}}{\partial \eps_j}(\boldsymbol{\eps})
\;>\; 0.
\]
Loosening any other player's budget enlarges $i$'s slack; tightening
it pushes $\Apld{i}$ toward the $(\eps_i,\delta)$-DP ceiling.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:decomp}: $A_{\eps_i,\delta}$ depends only on $\eps_i$,
so $\partial \mathrm{slack}_i / \partial \eps_j = -\partial \Apld{i} /
\partial \eps_j$, positive with the status of
\cref{prop:externality-full}: analytic at small $p$, certified beyond.
\end{proof}

This is the quantity the $(\eps_i,\delta,\Delta)$-iDP contract of
\citet{kaiser2026your, kaissis2024beyond} caps
(\cref{sec:mitigation}), and it has a group-size reading. Under
(Batch), a small group demanding strong privacy contributes little to
the expected batch, so the larger groups' sampling rates rise to
compensate: the minority \emph{exports} its privacy demand onto the
majority---the formal content of the observation in
\cite{kaiser2026your} that the \emph{proportion} of low-budget
records matters as much as their budgets.

\subsection{No symmetric equilibrium}
\label{sec:no-symmetric-ne}

If the externality must land on someone, one might still hope it lands
evenly. It cannot: in the fully symmetric two-player game the diagonal
is never an equilibrium.

\begin{proposition}[No symmetric pure \ac{NE}, symmetric 2-player \ac{PEG}]
\label[proposition]{prop:no-symmetric-ne}
Consider the two-player \ac{PEG} with $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = w > 0$ and $|G_1| =
|G_2|$. Assume $V' < 0$, that $A$, viewed as a function of
$(p,\sigma)$, is $C^2$ with $\partial A / \partial p > 0$ strictly on
the interior, and the responsiveness condition
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial A}{\partial p}
\;+\; \sigma\,\frac{\partial^2 A}{\partial p\,\partial\sigma}
\;-\; p\,\frac{\partial^2 A}{\partial p^2} \;>\; 0
\label{eq:sign-condition}
\end{equation}
at the diagonal operating point. Then no interior diagonal profile
$(\eps^\star, \eps^\star)$ with strict feasibility is a pure Nash
equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Change variables to $s_i := \sqrt{\rho(\eps_i,\delta)}$; by
\eqref{eq:sigma-implicit} the mechanism becomes an aggregator,
$\sigma = C/(s_1+s_2)$ and $p_i = C' s_i/(s_1+s_2)$: player $i$'s
sampling rate depends on their \emph{share} of the aggregate. At a
diagonal point the two first-order conditions collapse to one scalar
equation, and because $\sigma$ depends only on the sum $s_1+s_2$, the
shared-$V$ curvature cancels exactly between the own- and
cross-second derivatives; what survives is proportional to the left
side of \eqref{eq:sign-condition}
(\cref{eq:soc-difference}). Under the condition, this forces
$\partial^2 u_1/\partial s_1^2 > 0$ at the diagonal FOC: the candidate
is a strict local \emph{minimum} of each player's own utility, so both
players profit from deviating in either direction. Non-FOC diagonal
points fail the first-order test outright. Full proof in
\cref{app:proof-no-symmetric}.
\end{proof}

Condition \eqref{eq:sign-condition} is the sampling-dominates-noise
bracket of \cref{cor:externality-small-p} evaluated along the
diagonal: in the small-$p$ expansion its left side is
$I\sigma\,[\Adv_G(\sigma)/\sigma + \Adv_G'(\sigma)] + O(I^2 p) > 0$
by the positivity of the bracket in \eqref{eq:proof-bracket}, and it
holds numerically on every diagonal profile of the mechanism caches
of \cref{sec:experiments}. Two scope remarks. The statement is
interior: at the participation floor, or where sampling saturates
($p \to 1$ and $\partial A/\partial p$ degenerates), diagonal
equilibria can reappear---\cref{sec:experiments} meets exactly one
such corner. And only $V' < 0$ is used, so the impossibility is not
an artefact of our choice of $V$ (\cref{prop:V-invariance}).

The proposition is purely negative---it rules out the diagonal;
existence off the diagonal is a separate question
(\cref{sec:existence}). Because this claim carries the paper, we take
it apart: why deviating from symmetry pays, why the deviation does
not damp back, and what of symmetry survives. Each step is analytic
in origin and verified on the $K{=}200$ mechanism cache
(\cref{app:symbreak}).

\paragraph{Why deviate: differentiation has increasing returns.}
Constraint (Batch) conserves aggregate exposure,
$\sum_g |G_g|\,p_g = B$ at every profile: the game never decides
\emph{whether} a batch worth of sampling exposure is held, only by
whom and under how much noise. A player's sampling rate is their
\emph{share} of that conserved aggregate---by \eqref{eq:agg-form},
$p_i \propto s_i / (s_1{+}s_2)$, concave in their own action---while
their advantage is nearly linear in the rate
(\cref{cor:externality-small-p}). Concave exposure-taking plus linear
exposure-cost is exactly the combination that makes own utility
locally \emph{convex} at the symmetric first-order candidate
(\cref{eq:soc-difference}): from the diagonal, moving either way
pays. Loosening buys more model quality than the marginal exposure
costs; tightening sheds more exposure than the model quality it
forfeits; only conforming is locally worthless.
\Cref{fig:symbreak}(a) shows the trough on the mechanism cache, and
across all $200$ feasible diagonal profiles the best unilateral
deviation gains at least $1.9\times 10^{-3}$ utility.

\paragraph{Why it does not recover: budgets are strategic
substitutes.} A deviation, once made, lowers the other player's
return to imitating it---the budgets are strategic substitutes
through the shared batch: near the diagonal, the discrete
cross-difference of $u_1$ in $(\eps_1, \eps_2)$ is negative on
$80$--$91\%$ of diagonal cells at jitter-free strides
(\cref{app:symbreak}). Once the opponent has claimed the exposed role, the
noise gain is already banked and one's own marginal exposure only
adds cost, so the best response moves \emph{away} from the deviation,
not back toward it. Deviations from symmetry therefore amplify:
alternating best-response dynamics started at every feasible diagonal
profile of the $K{=}200$ grid leave the diagonal at the first
response and terminate at an off-diagonal pure equilibrium
(\cref{fig:symbreak}(b), $200$ of $200$ starts). Restoring symmetry
would require both players to cross the trough simultaneously, which
is individually irrational at every point along the way.

\paragraph{What survives: a lottery over roles.} The discretised game
is finite and symmetric, so a \emph{symmetric} equilibrium exists in
mixed strategies~\cite{nash1951noncooperative}---no contradiction
with \cref{prop:no-symmetric-ne}, which concerns pure profiles. The
game restricted to the two budgets of a mirror equilibrium pair is
literally Hawk--Dove (\cref{tab:hawkdove}): both players prefer the
protected role, the double-tight profile is the crash, and only the
asymmetric profiles are equilibria. The symmetric mixed equilibrium
is the classical chicken resolution: on the $K{=}22$ game it mixes
the two roles ($\eps = 2.52$ w.p.\ $0.76$, $\eps = 3.17$ w.p.\
$0.24$) for an expected utility of $0.304$, strictly between the
exposed ($0.296$) and protected ($0.335$) role payoffs. Symmetry thus
survives only \emph{ex ante}, as a fair lottery over asymmetric
outcomes; the draw still assigns the roles. And exactly as in
classical Hawk--Dove, where an asymmetry between the players settles
which convention is played~\cite{maynardsmith1973logic}, any
preference asymmetry resolves the selection problem here---that is
the content of \cref{prop:self-sorting}, to which we turn next.

\subsection{Who carries it: self-sorting}
\label{sec:self-sorting}

Under exact symmetry, which player concedes is decided by equilibrium
selection---the equilibria come in mirror pairs. Any preference
asymmetry breaks the tie, and it breaks it in a fixed direction.

\begin{proposition}[Self-sorting, 2-player \ac{PEG}]
\label[proposition]{prop:self-sorting}
Consider the 2-player \ac{PEG} with $\lambda_1 > \lambda_2 > 0$, $|G_1| = |G_2|$, $V$
strictly decreasing, own-budget monotonicity $\partial \Apld{i} / \partial
\eps_i > 0$, and the exchange condition (S) of \cref{app:proofs}
(satisfied in the high-noise regime and verified numerically at every
equilibrium candidate we report). Then every pure Nash equilibrium
$(\eps_1^\star, \eps_2^\star)$ satisfies $\eps_1^\star \le
\eps_2^\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Suppose the \emph{more} privacy-sensitive player held the higher
budget. Neither player may profit from swapping to the other's
budget; writing out the two no-swap inequalities and dividing them,
condition (S) forces $\lambda_2 \geq \lambda_1$---a contradiction. Full proof in
\cref{app:proof-self-sorting}.
\end{proof}

The direction is the counterintuitive part: the \emph{more}
privacy-sensitive player ends up with the lower budget \emph{and} the
lower realised advantage, and the realised risk lands on the player
who cares least. They are not being exploited---at equilibrium they are
compensated through the shared model quality---but the composition of
outcomes is exactly the one the \ac{iDP} contract never priced: your
realised protection is set by your neighbours' preferences, not your
own.

\subsection{Existence, and why enumeration is necessary}
\label{sec:existence}

\begin{theorem}[Pure \ac{NE} existence on a substitutes box, $n = 2$]
\label{thm:continuous-ne-smallp}
Let $\mathcal{B} = [\eps_{\mathrm{lo}}, \eps_{\mathrm{hi}}]^2$ be any box of
feasible profiles on which $p_g \leq p_\star$ (as in
\cref{cor:externality-small-p}) and the budgets are strategic
substitutes, $\partial^2 u_i / \partial \eps_1 \partial \eps_2 \leq
0$---in the small-$p$ expansion the latter needs only $V''(\sigma) +
2V'(\sigma)/\sigma \leq 0$, i.e.\ $\sigma \leq \sqrt 3$ for the
canonical $V$. Under the order that reverses one player's coordinate,
the \ac{PEG} restricted to $\mathcal{B}$ is a supermodular game on a compact lattice
and admits a non-empty complete lattice of pure Nash equilibria of
the restricted game, with extremal
elements~\cite{topkis1979equilibrium, milgrom1990rationalizability};
any such equilibrium whose best responses over the full action set
remain in $\mathcal{B}$ is an equilibrium of the \ac{PEG} itself.
\end{theorem}

The regime is the relevant one: \ac{DPSGD} sampling rates sit at
$10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$, and the noisier half of the caches' operating
range $\sigma \in [0.5, 5]$ satisfies the $V$-curvature condition.
Two honest flags remain. Existence on the full feasible region,
including the saturation corner $p_g \to 1$, is open; there we rely
on exhaustive grid enumeration (\cref{sec:experiments}). And the
lattice route is intrinsically two-player: substitutes cannot be
re-ordered into complements for every pair at once when $n > 2$. The
aggregative structure of \eqref{eq:sigma-implicit}---opponents enter
only through a sum---is the natural route to larger games, in the
Cournot tradition of \citet{novshek1985cournot}; we leave it open and
use enumeration and fictitious play at $n > 2$ (\cref{sec:H3}). Nor
is enumeration laziness: the familiar shortcut of minimising a
potential function is unavailable in principle.

\begin{proposition}[The \ac{PEG} is not a potential game]
\label[proposition]{prop:non-potential}
Under \eqref{eq:utility} and the mechanism of \cref{def:sampling-idp},
the \ac{PEG} admits no exact, weighted, or ordinal potential in the sense
of \citet{monderer1996potential}: the Monderer--Shapley cross-partial
condition fails for every choice of positive player weights, and the
discretised game contains a four-profile strict improvement cycle.
\end{proposition}

The Price-of-Privacy game was a potential game precisely because its
privacy cost was separable~\cite{pejo2019together}; the mechanism's
coupling is what destroys the structure (proof in \cref{app:proofs}).
This is why our existence route above trades concavity for
monotonicity, and why the sign analysis of
\cref{cor:externality-small-p,prop:externality-full} is the
load-bearing input.

For the numerics we also record the best-response geometry. At an
interior best response, $\partial u_i / \partial \eps_i = 0$ reads
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\lambda_i}
\;=\; \frac{ \partial \Apld{i} / \partial \eps_i }
          { V'(\sigma)\;\partial \sigma / \partial \eps_i }
\;\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\eps}},
\label{eq:foc}
\end{equation}
defining, for each player, an implicit \ac{BR} curve in the action plane
whose intersections are the pure \acp{NE}. Neither side of \eqref{eq:foc}
has a closed form, but both are computable by single \ac{PLD} evaluations,
which is how \cref{sec:experiments} traces the \ac{BR} loci.

\subsection{Coalitions: the attack is a corollary}
\label{sec:coalition}

\begin{corollary}[Coalition best response recovers the attack]
\label[corollary]{cor:coalition-attack}
Fix a target $t$, a coalition $C \subseteq N \setminus \{t\}$ of size
$k$, and any actions $\boldsymbol{\eps}_{-C}$ for the others. As
$\kappa \to \infty$ in \cref{def:coalition}, the coalition's
Stackelberg best response is the coalition-symmetric profile at the
\emph{participation-preserving minimum} budget
$\eps^{\star}_k(\boldsymbol{\eps}_{-C})$, and the target advantage
$\Apld{t}$ induced at that best response is non-decreasing in $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
$\partial \Apld{t} / \partial \eps_i < 0$ for every $i \in C$
(\cref{prop:externality-full}, with its analytic-plus-certified
status), so the coalition pushes every budget as
low as feasibility admits; below the participation floor a colluder
drops out of (Batch) and stops exerting the externality, hence the
\emph{infimum feasible} rather than the grid minimum. Adding a member
enlarges the set of budgets pulling $\sigma$ up, so $\Apld{t}$ is monotone
in $k$.
\end{proof}

This is the budget-manipulation attack of \citet{kaiser2026your},
recovered as rational play: any coordinated subset of data holders
whose joint utility weights the target's harm arrives at the same
action vector, whether or not anyone calls it an attack. The corollary
deliberately claims no more than that mapping---the empirical
demonstration is prior work's---and \cref{sec:attacks} shows its
force is contingent on a target that does not respond.

\paragraph{The role of $V$ is only its sign.} Every load-bearing
statement above reads $V$ solely through the sign of $V'$: the
externality proofs never touch $V$, the coalition corollary uses only
the externality sign on the target's coordinate, and the
best-response condition \eqref{eq:foc} depends on $V$ only through
$\mathrm{sgn}\,V'$. We record this formally as
\cref{prop:V-invariance} (\cref{app:V}), where an ablation over six
admissible shapes---from Gaussian to affine---also confirms the
equilibrium structure numerically (\cref{app:v-shape}). The
experimental choice $V(\sigma) = 1/(1+\sigma^2)$ is thus a
representative of a class, not a load-bearing assumption.
