\section{The Privacy Externality Game}
\label{sec:game}

We formalise the strategic situation faced by data contributors under
sampling-based \ac{iDP} as a normal-form game on a continuous action space.
The formalisation deliberately follows the two-player
collaborative-learning template of \citet{pejo2019together}, with one
central modification: the privacy cost is no longer separable across
players, because the mechanism couples it.

\subsection{Players, actions, and utilities}

\begin{definition}[Privacy Externality Game]
\label[definition]{def:peg}
Fix a common $\delta>0$, an expected batch size $B$, an iteration
count $I$, and a partition of the dataset into $n$ groups of sizes
$|G_1|,\dots,|G_n|$. The \acf{PEG} is the tuple
$\langle N, \Sigma, U\rangle$ where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N = \{1,\dots,n\}$ is the set of players. We adopt the
  federated-silo granularity of \cite{kaiser2026your}: player $i$ is
  the entity controlling group $i$. Each player picks a single scalar
  budget for their group; per-record budget distributions within a group
  are an extension we discuss in \cref{sec:discussion}.
\item Each player's action set is $\Sigma_i = [\eps^{\min},
  \eps^{\max}] \subset \R_{>0}$.
\item Given the action profile $\boldsymbol{\eps} \in \prod_i
  \Sigma_i$, the mechanism solution $(\sigma(\boldsymbol{\eps}),
  p_1(\boldsymbol{\eps}),\dots, p_n(\boldsymbol{\eps}))$ is determined
  by \cref{def:sampling-idp}.
\item Each player's utility is
  \begin{equation}
  u_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \;=\;
  V(\sigma(\boldsymbol{\eps}))
  \;-\;
  \lambda_i\cdot \Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps}),
  \label{eq:utility}
  \end{equation}
  where $\lambda_i > 0$ is the player's utility/privacy weight of
  \cref{sec:idp}, pricing a unit of realised \ac{MIA} advantage on
  their own data against a unit of model quality.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Vulnerability function]
\label[definition]{def:vulnerability}
The \emph{vulnerability function} of player $i$ is $\Apld{i}\colon
\prod_i\Sigma_i \to [0, 1]$, $\boldsymbol{\eps} \mapsto
\Adv\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{p_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}),\,\sigma(\boldsymbol{\eps}),\,I}\bigr)$,
the tight analytical \ac{MIA} advantage~\eqref{eq:adv} of the $I$-fold
$(p_i, \sigma)$-subsampled Gaussian.
\end{definition}

In the hierarchy \eqref{eq:hierarchy}, $\Apld{i}$ is a level-2 object:
the mechanism-specific supremum over all adversaries against the
mechanism actually solved for at the profile---a true bound on what
any attacker can extract there, unlike the mechanism-agnostic level-1
ceiling written in player $i$'s contract, and unlike the outcome of
any particular attack.
It is the advantage measure of \citet{kaiser2026your} transferred
unchanged to a strategic setting. Its dependence on
$\boldsymbol{\eps}$ runs entirely through $(p_i, \sigma)$, both of
which the mechanism's constraints (Budget) and (Batch) determine from
the whole budget vector; this coupling is the externality.

\paragraph{The model-quality term.} For $V$ we use
\begin{equation}
V(\sigma) \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + \sigma^2},
\label{eq:V}
\end{equation}
monotone-decreasing in the noise multiplier and bounded in $[0,1]$.
The shape is motivated by the excess-empirical-risk bound of
\citet{bassily2014private} for convex DP-ERM, which under the moments
accountant of \citet{abadi2016deep} translates into a $\sigma^2$
dependence of the excess risk (\cref{app:V}); in the potentially
non-convex deep-learning regime we use it as a directional proxy,
consistent with the monotone accuracy degradation reported
empirically~\cite{tramer2021private, abadi2016deep, boenisch2023have}.
None of our results hinge on this choice:
\cref{prop:V-invariance} shows that every load-bearing statement holds
for any $V$ that is continuous, strictly decreasing, and bounded on
the feasible noise range, and \cref{app:v-shape} verifies numerically
that the equilibrium structure is unchanged under six qualitatively
different shapes.

\begin{assumption}[Participation]
\label[assumption]{ass:participation}
A profile $\boldsymbol{\eps}$ is \emph{feasible} iff the mechanism
solution satisfies $p_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) > 0$ for every player.
Profiles with $p_i = 0$ correspond to a player whose data never enters
training; their contract holds trivially and we exclude such profiles,
restricting each action set to the participating region.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Contracted versus excess cost}
\label{sec:decomposition}

The slack of \cref{sec:mia}, instantiated per player, separates the
vulnerability into a contracted part and a played part:
\begin{equation}
\Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \;=\; A_{\eps_i,\delta}
- \mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}),
\qquad
\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \;\geq\; 0.
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The two terms have different owners. The level-1 ceiling
$A_{\eps_i,\delta}$ depends on player $i$'s budget alone---it is
written in their contract---while the slack is a function of the
entire profile. The externality therefore lives exclusively between
levels 1 and 2 of \eqref{eq:hierarchy}: when others tighten their
budgets, $\mathrm{slack}_i$ shrinks and $\Apld{i}$ climbs toward the
ceiling (\cref{prop:slack-monotonicity}); the $\Delta$-clause of
\cref{sec:mitigation} caps exactly this movable term.

\subsection{Coalition variant}

\begin{definition}[$k$-coalition Stackelberg PEG]
\label[definition]{def:coalition}
Fix a target $t \in N$ and a colluding subset $C \subset
N\setminus\{t\}$ with $|C| = k$. The $k$-coalition Stackelberg \ac{PEG} is
the two-stage game in which (i) the coalition jointly commits to an
action vector $\boldsymbol{\eps}_C$ maximising the augmented utility
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i \in C} u_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \;+\;
\kappa\, \Apld{t}(\boldsymbol{\eps}),
\label{eq:coalition-util}
\end{equation}
with \emph{malice weight} $\kappa \geq 0$ on the target's
vulnerability~\cite{pejo2019together}, and (ii) the remaining players
best-respond. $\kappa = 0$ recovers the benign \ac{PEG}; $\kappa \to
\infty$ recovers the budget-manipulation threat model of
\citet{kaiser2026your}.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Equilibrium concepts and information}

We use pure Nash equilibrium on the feasible region as the primary
solution concept. Existence on the continuous action space is not
automatic---$u_i$ is not concave in $\eps_i$, so the classical
quasi-concavity route~\cite{rosen1965existence, glicksberg1952further}
does not apply; \cref{sec:existence} gives an order-theoretic
existence result in the practically relevant regime. Numerically we
enumerate pure \acp{NE} by brute force on a dense log-spaced grid
$\mathcal{A}_K \subset [\eps^{\min}, \eps^{\max}]$ (a finite game, so
a mixed \ac{NE} exists in any case~\cite{nash1950equilibrium}) and use
fictitious play~\cite{monderer1996potential} where enumeration is out
of reach.

\begin{remark}[Awareness is information, not utility]
\label[remark]{rem:awareness}
Every player has the same utility \eqref{eq:utility}; what separates
them is the level of the hierarchy \eqref{eq:hierarchy} at which they
can price its cost term. A \emph{nominal-information} player sees
only their own contract and therefore prices at level 1, taking the
ceiling $A_{\eps_i,\delta}$ as their cost. That ceiling depends on
their own budget alone, so their best response ignores
$\boldsymbol{\eps}_{-i}$: they play the separable Price-of-Privacy
game of \citet{pejo2019together} unchanged. An \emph{aware} player
can compute $\Apld{i}(\boldsymbol{\eps})$ from the public mechanism
parameters, prices at level 2, and plays the \ac{PEG}. The
distinction matters for the attack analysis of \cref{sec:attacks}:
the known attacks succeed against nominal-information targets, and
awareness alone turns out to be a substantial defence.
\end{remark}
