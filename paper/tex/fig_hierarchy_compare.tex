\caption{\textbf{Advantage hierarchy across contract points} (rows
$(\eps,\delta) = (10,10^{-12})$, $(2,10^{-12})$, $(8,10^{-5})$; all
$I = 1$). Each row repeats the construction of \cref{fig:hierarchy} at a
different contract: the six subsampled-Gaussian mechanisms are recalibrated
so their profiles pass through that row's point (orange dot), and the
mechanism-agnostic bound $A_{\eps,\delta}$ (orange bar) is that contract's
worst case. The contract point sets both the envelope and the achievable
spread: at $(2,10^{-12})$ even the Gaussian reaches only $A_{\mathrm{PLD}} =
0.12$ against an envelope of $0.76$; at $(8,10^{-5})$ the larger $\delta$
lifts the Gaussian to $0.60$ and the envelope to $\approx 1$. In every case
the realised advantages sit well below the $(\eps,\delta)$ bound.}
