\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have shown that sampling-based individual differential privacy is not
just \emph{vulnerable} to collusion attacks but \emph{equilibrium-asymmetric}.
The pure Nash equilibria of the Privacy Externality Game, when they exist on
the dense action grid, sit \emph{off the diagonal}: one player accepts a
higher realised MIA advantage and is compensated by a higher model-utility
benefit; the other absorbs the opposite trade. The budget-manipulation
attack of \citet{kaiser2026your} is precisely the asymmetric equilibrium
played by a coalition that weights the target's harm in its joint utility,
and \cref{cor:coalition-attack} shows this is the rational best response of
\emph{any} coordinated subset of data holders.

\paragraph{Limitations of this work.} Three caveats are worth noting.
First, our model assumes one-shot full-information play. Real
deployments of iDP are dynamic: contributors join and leave; budgets are
re-negotiated; trainers re-train. A natural extension is a repeated game
in which the players also have private preference types and learn about
one another across rounds. \Cref{sec:br-peg} formalises such a game as
the \emph{Bayesian Repeated PEG} (BR-PEG), states a sharpened attack
characterisation (\cref{prop:bayesian-not-min}) that identifies the
optimal adversarial budget as strictly interior to the feasible action
grid, and reports a small simulation. We treat the fully two-sided
repeated game of \citet{aumann1995repeated} and quantitative regret
rates in the style of \citet{balcan2015commitment} as follow-up work.
Second, we assume full information about other players' actions. Real
contracts may keep budgets private, and \cref{rem:awareness} already
distinguishes between fully aware players and players who only see their
nominal $(\eps_i,\delta)$ contract. A Bayesian PEG that treats $\eps_i$ as
a private type drawn from a known distribution is a natural and important
extension.
Third, our analysis is restricted to the sampling-based realisation of iDP.
The sensitivity-based realisation~\cite{boenisch2023have} adjusts per-sample
clipping bounds rather than per-sample sampling rates and does not exhibit
the same externality (the noise-to-sensitivity ratio is preserved by
construction). Our framework specialises to that case as the trivial PEG in
which the externality vanishes.

\subsection{Repeated Bayesian variant (BR-PEG)}
\label{sec:br-peg}

The one-shot analysis leaves the strategic content of repetition and
incomplete information untouched. We formalise the natural extension---
the \emph{Bayesian Repeated PEG}---and identify the qualitative
consequence of the mechanism's leader--follower coupling: the optimal
adversarial budget is bounded away from the minimum feasible one.

\paragraph{The game.}
Two players, $H$ (honest) and $M$ (malicious). At $t=0$, $H$ draws a
private type $\theta_H = (w_H, n_H)$ from a finite set $\Theta$ known
to $M$. Per round $t \in \{1,\dots,T\}$: (i) $M$ observes the history
and picks $(\eps_M^t, n_M^t)$; (ii) $H$ observes $(\eps_M^t, n_M^t)$
and myopically best-responds with $\eps_H^t \in \mathcal{A}$ that
maximises the one-shot utility $u_H(\eps_H; \eps_M^t, \theta_H) =
V(\sigma) - w_H A_H$, with $(\sigma, p_H, p_M, A_H, A_M)$ returned by
the sampling-based iDP mechanism of \cref{def:sampling-idp}; (iii) the
pair $(\eps_H^t, u_H^t)$ becomes public and $M$ updates a posterior
$\pi^t \in \Delta(\Theta)$ over $\theta_H$ via the observation
likelihood induced by $H$'s best-response map. $M$'s total score is
$\sum_t A_H^t$. Positioned in the literature, the game is the
Stackelberg-learning template of \citet{letchford2009learning,
blum2014learning, balcan2015commitment} with the tabular follower
payoff replaced by the DP-accountant coupling of
\cref{def:sampling-idp}, and it is the myopic-informed special case of
the one-sided incomplete-information repeated game of
\citet{aumann1995repeated}. The prior $\pi^0$ has full support on
$\Theta$, so Kalai--Lehrer's absolute-continuity condition holds
trivially and posterior consistency is automatic; the interesting
content is what $M$ does once consistency kicks in.

\begin{proposition}[Bayesian-greedy attack is not min-budget]
\label{prop:bayesian-not-min}
Consider the BR-PEG with $H$ myopic and $M$ posterior-greedy on a
finite action grid $\mathcal{A} \subset [\eps^{\min}, \eps^{\max}]$.
Fix a type $\theta_H^\star \in \Theta$ and let
\[
G(\eps_M; \theta) \;:=\; A_H\!\bigl(\eps_M,\, \eps_H^{\mathrm{BR}}(\eps_M; \theta)\bigr)
\]
be the composed map from $M$'s action to the elicited advantage, where
$\eps_H^{\mathrm{BR}}(\eps_M; \theta) \in \arg\max_{\eps_H \in
\mathcal{A}} u_H(\eps_H; \eps_M, \theta)$. Assume
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\normalfont(I)] \emph{Identifiability.} The observation
likelihood is $\theta_H^\star$-identifying on the equilibrium path in
the sense of \citet{kalai1993rational}: for a full-support prior
$\pi^0$, $\pi^t(\theta_H^\star) \to 1$ $\pi^0$-a.s.
\item[\normalfont(A)] \emph{Interior argmax.} $G(\cdot; \theta_H^\star)$
attains its maximum on $\mathcal{A}$ at a unique
$\eps_M^\star(\theta_H^\star) \in \mathcal{A}$ with
$\eps_M^\star(\theta_H^\star) > \eps^{\min}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the posterior-greedy action
$\eps_M^{\mathrm{greedy}}(t) \in \arg\max_{\eps_M \in \mathcal{A}}
\mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim \pi^t}\!\bigl[G(\eps_M; \theta)\bigr]$
satisfies
$\eps_M^{\mathrm{greedy}}(t) = \eps_M^\star(\theta_H^\star)$
eventually, $\pi^0$-a.s.; equivalently, setting
$\eta := \eps_M^\star(\theta_H^\star) - \eps^{\min} > 0$,
$\eps_M^{\mathrm{greedy}}(t) \geq \eps^{\min} + \eta$ for all
sufficiently large $t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By (I), $\pi^t \to \delta_{\theta_H^\star}$ in total variation. Since
$G$ is bounded ($0 \leq A_H \leq 1$), the posterior-averaged score
$\bar G(\eps_M; \pi^t) := \mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim \pi^t} G(\eps_M;
\theta)$ satisfies
$|\bar G(\eps_M; \pi^t) - G(\eps_M; \theta_H^\star)| \leq 1 -
\pi^t(\theta_H^\star) \to 0$, uniformly over the finite grid
$\mathcal{A}$. By (A), the truth-argmax gap
$\Delta := G(\eps_M^\star; \theta_H^\star) - \max_{\eps_M \neq
\eps_M^\star} G(\eps_M; \theta_H^\star) > 0$; once
$1 - \pi^t(\theta_H^\star) < \Delta/2$, the argmax of
$\bar G(\cdot; \pi^t)$ coincides with $\eps_M^\star(\theta_H^\star)$
for every subsequent round. \Cref{app:bayesian} works the argument
through in full.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical realisation and scope.} For the demo instance of
\cref{app:bayesian} ($\Theta = \{w_H \in \{0.5, 1, 2\}\}$, $n_H = n_M
= 5000$, $H$'s true type $w_H^\star = 2$), the posterior collapses at
$t = 1$, and from then on the Bayesian-greedy attack plays
$\eps_M^\star \approx 3.08$ and elicits $A_H \approx 0.454$---roughly
twice the naive-minimum attack $\eps_M = 1$ and fifty times the
benign baseline $\eps_M = 64$. The separation from the naive-attack
anchor is $\eta \approx 2.08$; assumption (A) is verified numerically
on the mechanism grid. Two extensions sit outside this scope. First,
the strategising-$H$ case, in which $H$ chooses her action sequence to
manage what she reveals: a two-sided incomplete-information game in
the sense of \citet{aumann1995repeated}. Second, a regret rate in the
style of \citet{balcan2015commitment, blum2014learning} that quantifies
the number of rounds until $\eps_M^{\mathrm{greedy}}(t) =
\eps_M^\star$; the finite-type Kalai--Lehrer setup used here delivers
one-round identification and so provides only a constant-regret upper
bound. Both extensions fit the framework above without modification.

\subsection{Modelling choices, mitigation, and open questions}

\paragraph{The role of the model-utility function $V$.} Our choice $V(\sigma)
= 1/(1+\sigma^2)$ is motivated by the convex DP-ERM excess-risk bound of
\citet{bassily2014private}, and we use it as a directional proxy in our
possibly non-convex setting. The qualitative findings---existence of
asymmetric NEs, monotone coalition attack, externality lemma---do not depend
on the precise shape of $V$; they require only that $V$ is continuous,
monotone-decreasing in $\sigma$, and bounded. We expect the same qualitative
picture under any utility function with these three properties.

\paragraph{Mitigation.} The natural mitigation is to make the contract
internalise the externality. The $(\eps_i, \delta, \Delta)$-iDP formulation
of \citet{kaissis2024beyond} adds a $\Delta$-divergence bound on the realised
vulnerability, restricting the action space of the PEG to profiles that
satisfy a per-record bound on excess advantage.
\Cref{prop:delta-neutralisation} makes the deterministic half of this
statement precise: capping the slack at $\Delta$ squeezes every player's
realised advantage into $[\bar c(\eps_i)-\Delta, \bar c(\eps_i)]$ and
caps its cross-profile variation at fixed $\eps_i$ by $\Delta$. Existence
of a unique equilibrium under this restriction is left to future work
(the second open question below).

\begin{proposition}[$\Delta$-neutralisation of the externality]
\label{prop:delta-neutralisation}
Restrict the action set to profiles on which
$\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps})\leq\Delta$ for every player $i$
(equivalently: the $(\eps_i,\delta,\Delta)$-iDP contract of
\citet{kaissis2024beyond} is enforced by the mechanism). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Pointwise squeeze.} At every feasible profile
$\boldsymbol{\eps}$ in the restricted action set,
\[
\bar c(\eps_i)-\Delta
\;\leq\;A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps})
\;\leq\;\bar c(\eps_i).
\]
\item[(ii)] \emph{Cross-profile bound at fixed own budget.} For any two
feasible profiles $\boldsymbol{\eps},\boldsymbol{\eps}'$ in the
restricted action set that agree in $i$'s own coordinate ($\eps_i =
\eps_i'$),
\[
\bigl|A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps})-A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}')\bigr|\;\leq\;\Delta.
\]
\end{enumerate}
That is: once the slack is capped by $\Delta$, no unilateral or joint
deviation of the other players can move player $i$'s realised advantage
by more than $\Delta$ from any other feasible value it can attain at the
same $\eps_i$; and $A_i$ never falls below its DP worst case by more
than $\Delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both parts use only the identity~\eqref{eq:decomp} and the
non-negativity of $\mathrm{slack}_i$.

\emph{(i) Pointwise squeeze.} By definition
$\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps})\geq 0$, so
$A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) = \bar c(\eps_i) -
\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \leq \bar c(\eps_i)$. The
restriction $\mathrm{slack}_i\leq\Delta$ then gives
$A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) \geq \bar c(\eps_i) - \Delta$.

\emph{(ii) Cross-profile bound.} For two profiles with
$\eps_i = \eps_i'$, $\bar c(\eps_i)$ cancels and
\[
A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) - A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}')
\;=\;\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}')-\mathrm{slack}_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}).
\]
Both slacks lie in $[0,\Delta]$ by the restriction and by non-negativity,
hence the difference lies in $[-\Delta,\Delta]$ and
$|A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}) - A_i(\boldsymbol{\eps}')|\leq\Delta$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Open questions.} The following questions naturally arise from
this work but lie outside its scope:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does a pure-strategy NE always survive on the continuous action space
under the coupled cost, and does a $\Delta$-bound on the slack suffice for
existence?
\item Is the PEG a potential game in the sense of \cite{monderer1996potential}?
PoP's analysis was a potential game because the privacy cost was
separable; the externality term breaks this directly. Conditions on the
cross-partial of $A_i$ that recover the potential structure would unify
the two settings.
\item What is the sign of the externality across composition regimes
(minority vs majority weak-privacy)? Our experiments suggest the smaller
group is favoured, but the analytic dependence on $|G_g|$ deserves a
closed-form characterisation.
\item What is the appropriate player granularity (single sample versus
silo) in the FL setting, and how does the granularity change the
equilibria?
\item Worst-case (analytical) vs realised (empirical) cost---which should
the contract be written against, and how large is the gap in practice
across deep-learning workloads?
\item Is the $(\eps_i, \delta, \Delta)$-iDP contract by itself sufficient
as a collusion-resistant mechanism, or are sensitivity-based iDP or
public-data padding needed when the slack is exceeded?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Wider lesson.} The wider lesson is that an \emph{individual}
privacy contract is incomplete: it does not pin down the collective
dynamics. Where individuals interact strategically (in a single
machine-learning training, in a federated round, in any future composition
of the iDP mechanism), the contract must also constrain their joint
behaviour or the equilibrium will quietly violate at least one
participant's expectations while preserving every formal guarantee.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} The promise of iDP---that every data contributor
controls her own privacy---is broken by the privacy externality of
sampling-based mechanisms. We formalised the strategic situation faced by
iDP data contributors as the Privacy Externality Game, proved monotonicity
of the externality and characterised the equilibria. The result is that
the collusion attack on iDP is not adversarial but \emph{rational}, and the
remedy must operate at the level of the contract.
